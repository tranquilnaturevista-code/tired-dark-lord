## What it does

`main.py` writes one original monologue of exactly 100 words to
`/output/entry.txt`:

> Hero. Stand there. Lower the torch, would you? The light... my eyes.
>
> I am Varric, lord of the Ninth Toll. Forty years I have kept this throne. Tolls and taxes and traitors at the gate, and none of them ever knocked. They all just... burst in. Like you. Swords first, manners never.
>
> Want the crown? Take it. It pinches. Velvet moth eaten, gold thin, and the weight of it... gods, it sits right here, behind my eyes.
>
> Before you swing, hero. One thing. The barge leaves at dawn. The dead will not row. Kill me tonight, and you must row.

The monologue is written by hand for this bounty. The speaker is a
specific villain — Varric, lord of the Ninth Toll, forty years on a
throne he won by collecting tolls, taxes and traitors at the gate —
with a scheme still running: the dawn barge, and the drowned dead
who row it. The tiredness is never announced; it lives in the
pauses, the sentences that trail off, and the small deflections —
a torch held too high, a crown that pinches, a weight behind the
eyes. The sting lands in the last line: kill him, and the hero
inherits the chore.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py   # writes /output/entry.txt (or $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt)
```

Deterministic: the same monologue on every run. The output is 536
characters of UTF-8 text (536 bytes, plain ASCII) in exactly 100
words, within the 1–600 character gate.
